## Summary

Samples held in the ring buffer survive a reset or power loss, so a unit that browns out in the field no longer loses the readings that explain why. Sending samples off the device is left out.

## User stories

S1. As a field engineer, I want the samples taken just before a reset to still be there after it, so that I can see what the sensor read when the unit failed.

## Must have

| ID | Requirement | Why / source | Verified by |
|---|---|---|---|
| SAMPLELOG-1 | After a reset or power loss, every sample taken more than 1 s before it is still in the buffer, in the order it was taken | S1. Three of the last five field returns had an empty buffer after a brown-out | Test that resets mid-run on `native_sim` |
| SAMPLELOG-2 | Persistence holds for 10 years of continuous sampling at 10 samples per second | Product service life, decided by Mira 2026-09-29 | Calculation in the design, checked against the storage datasheet |
| SAMPLELOG-3 | `zl_ringbuf_put` stays safe to call from an ISR and never blocks | The sensor reader calls it from its data-ready interrupt on the production board | Existing ringbuf_log suite plus a timing test |

## Should have

## Won't have

| ID | Item | Why not now |
|---|---|---|
| SAMPLELOG-4 | Uploading samples off the device | The production board has no radio in this hardware revision |

## Context

The production board's `storage_partition` is 64 KB of NOR flash in 4 KB erase sectors, rated for 10,000 erase cycles per sector (flash datasheet, table 7). The board has 64 KB of RAM, of which 18 KB is free in the current build (measured with `west build -t ram_report`).
